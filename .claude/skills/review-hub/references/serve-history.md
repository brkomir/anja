## What the model rule replaced, and the same-day correction

**What this replaced, and what it cost (2026-08-16):** the routing policy routed all breaker/`--code` lenses to Fable, so this hub dispatched **seven Fable lenses in one session, five and six at a time, every one inheriting max effort** — the most expensive configuration available. The complaint: the metered top tier was being used up too fast to leave room for other work, and max effort on reviews was overkill. **Three compounding misses:** the model clause was applied BY ROLE with its cost never priced; `effort` was never passed in any of the seven dispatches (the knob was in every call); and builder tier was never checked before dispatch, though the asymmetry clause already required it.

⛔ **CORRECTION, SAME DAY — THE CLAUSE ABOVE ORIGINALLY ENDED *"on one arc the builder was Opus, so the Fable lenses were straightforwardly below tier."* THAT IS THE TIER ORDER INVERTED. FABLE IS THE TOP TIER; OPUS IS THE STEP DOWN.** Fable lenses on an Opus-built commit sit **ABOVE** builder tier, so the asymmetry rule **HELD on both arcs**. Three independent lines in the model-routing policy settle the direction: *"Uncertain classification → inherit (Fable). **Down-route** only on a confident verify-vs-generate call"* (down-route FROM Fable); the routing table putting breakers on Fable under *"the top tier goes where errors are least catchable"*; and the exhaustion precedent *"Re-dispatched on **Opus** — equal tier to the builders"* (Opus is the step down, and it only held there because those builders were Opus).

**So the real defect was the OPPOSITE of the one first recorded: the panel was OVER-provisioned, not under.** Which is exactly what the complaint said: too much, never too little.

⛔ **THEREFORE: THIS RULE ROUTES PANELS TO OPUS FOR **COST**, NEVER FOR TIER SUFFICIENCY.** Fable is the scarce metered tier; sending panels to Opus deliberately spends review strength to buy back Fable headroom. **A reader who takes the struck clause at face value concludes a Fable panel is too WEAK and re-runs it at Opus — a downgrade, paid for.** That is not hypothetical: a peer read the inverted line, believed it, and told this hub its lenses were below tier and owed an Opus re-run. **One error, two carriers, and the second asserted it as derived.** What survives from the original miss: the tier check was never run. What does not: any claim about what it would have shown.

⚠️ **The shape worth carrying past this incident: neither seat checked an ordering while answering a question whose entire subject was that ordering.** The three settling lines were in the file both of us had open.
