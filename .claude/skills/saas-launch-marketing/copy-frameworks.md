# Copy Frameworks

Conversion copywriting templates and formulas for SaaS marketing. The agent reads this when writing landing pages, emails, social posts, or ad copy.

## The One-Sentence Test

Every piece of copy should pass this test: "If the reader only sees this one sentence, do they understand what we do and why they should care?"

## Headline Formulas

**Source:** Corey Haines, marketingskills (MIT), copywriting skill's copy-frameworks reference, 2026 — the headline formulas below are adapted from it; two of the formula lines match it once markup is set aside.

### Outcome + Pain Removal
```
{Achieve desirable outcome} without {pain point}
```
- "Get honest photo critique without the ego"
- "Know exactly what to fix without second-guessing"

### Feature + Audience
```
{Key capability} for {specific audience}
```
- "AI-powered editing guidance for serious photographers"
- "Structured critique for photographers who care about improving"

### Transformation
```
Turn {input/current state} into {desired outcome}
```
- "Turn guesswork into deliberate editing decisions"
- "Turn scattered feedback into a clear action plan"

### Never Again
```
Never {unpleasant thing} again
```
- "Never wonder 'is this edit making it worse?' again"
- "Never over-edit a good photo again"

### Pain Question
```
{Question that makes them feel the pain}?
```
- "Still tweaking sliders and hoping for the best?"
- "How many hours did you spend on edits that made it worse?"

### Anti-Positioning
```
Not {what everyone else says}. {What you actually do}.
```
- "Not another 'nice photo!' app. Structured critique that actually helps."

### Social Proof
```
{Number} {people} use {product} to {outcome}
```
- "[N] photographers use [Product] to refine less and choose better"

## Subheadline Patterns

The subheadline should add specificity to the headline:

- **Mechanism:** How it works in one sentence
- **Proof:** A concrete metric or result
- **Expansion:** Flesh out the benefit with more detail

Examples:
- Headline: "Know what to fix next."
  Subheadline: "Upload a photo, choose your context, get a structured critique with editor-specific adjustments."

- Headline: "Stop editing in circles."
  Subheadline: "AI identifies the single highest-leverage fix and tells you when further editing would hurt more than help."

## CTA Button Copy

**Source:** Corey Haines, marketingskills (MIT), copywriting and signup-flow skills, 2026 — the CTA formula and the five weak CTAs below are adapted from the copywriting skill, and the trust lines under Supporting Text echo the signup-flow skill.

### Formula: [Action Verb] + [What They Get]

**Good:**
- "Upload your first photo"
- "Get your first one free"
- "Start refining"
- "See a sample critique"
- "Try it free — no signup"

**Bad:**
- "Submit"
- "Sign Up"
- "Learn More"
- "Get Started"
- "Click Here"

### Supporting Text Under CTAs
- "[Price point] on the [plan name] plan"
- "No credit card required"
- "Takes 30 seconds"
- "Join [N]+ photographers"

## Landing Page Section Copy

**Source:** Corey Haines, marketingskills (MIT), copywriting skill, 2026 — the problem, solution, How It Works, benefit, testimonial and FAQ structures below are adapted from its section-writing tips, the problem section's opener included.

### Problem Section

Structure: Identify with the pain → Describe the specific frustrations → Hint at the cost.

```
You know the feeling.

You've been editing for an hour. The image looked better 20 minutes ago,
but you can't remember which adjustment broke it. You post it to a forum
and get "nice colors!" or conflicting opinions from strangers.

The problem isn't your eye — it's the feedback loop.
```

### Solution Section

Structure: Bridge from problem → Introduce your approach → Explain why it's different.

```
[Product] replaces vague opinions with structured critique.

Upload a photo, choose your context (portfolio, competition, Instagram, print),
and get a detailed breakdown: one bottleneck, specific slider adjustments for
YOUR editor, and a clear answer to "should I keep refining or move on?"
```

### How It Works

Always 3 steps. Each step: number + action + outcome.

```
1. Upload your photo
   Drag and drop. We read the EXIF data so the AI knows your camera settings.

2. Choose your lane
   Portfolio? Competition? Instagram? The critique adapts to what matters for your context.

3. Get your critique
   One bottleneck. Editor-specific adjustments. A clear verdict: refine or move on.
```

### Benefit Blocks

Structure: Benefit headline → 2-3 sentences of supporting copy → Optional proof point.

```
One bottleneck, not ten vague observations.

Most critique gives you a list of everything that could be better. That's
overwhelming and unhelpful. [Product] identifies the single highest-leverage
fix — the one change that moves the needle most.
```

### Testimonial Format

Always include: quote, name, role/context, specific result if possible.

```
"I was about to spend another hour on an edit that was already done.
 [Product] told me to stop. That was the most valuable feedback I've gotten."
 — [Name], landscape photographer
```

### FAQ Section

Address real objections. Each answer should be 2-3 sentences max.

Common objections for SaaS:
- "Is the AI actually good?" → Share methodology, accuracy, or comparison
- "Why should I pay when I can get critique on Reddit?" → Speed, specificity, consistency
- "Will this work for my genre/style?" → Explain lanes/contexts
- "What if I disagree with the AI?" → It's a tool, not a boss. Frame as a second opinion.
- "Is my data safe?" → Direct, simple answer about privacy

## Email Copy

### Welcome Email (Post-Signup)

Subject: "You're in. Here's what happens next"

```
Hey [name],

Welcome to [Product]. Here's what you can do right now:

→ [Primary CTA: Upload your first photo]

You get [X] free [uses] per [time period]. No credit card, no catch.

If you have questions, just reply to this email. I read every one.

— [Your name]
```

### Launch Day Email

Subject: "[Product] is live — here's your link"

```
Hey [name],

It's here.

[Product] is officially live. You were one of the first [X] people
to sign up, and I'm genuinely grateful.

Here's what's new since you last saw it:
• [Key improvement 1]
• [Key improvement 2]
• [Key improvement 3]

→ [CTA: Try it now]

If you like it, I'd be incredibly grateful if you shared it with
one photographer friend. Word of mouth is everything for a small team.

— [Your name]
```

## Social Post Templates

### X/Twitter Launch Thread

```
Post 1 (Hook):
I just launched [Product] — [one sentence value prop].

Here's the story of why I built it: 🧵

Post 2 (Problem):
Every photographer knows this feeling:
- You've been editing for an hour
- The image looked better 20 minutes ago
- You post for feedback and get "nice colors!" or conflicting opinions

Post 3 (Solution):
So I built [Product].

It does one thing: tells you what to fix next, and when to stop.

[Screenshot of the product in action]

Post 4 (How it works):
How it works:
1. Upload a photo
2. Choose your context (portfolio, competition, print, etc.)
3. Get a structured critique with editor-specific adjustments

Post 5 (Differentiator):
What makes it different:
- One bottleneck, not ten vague observations
- Actual slider ranges for Lightroom, Capture One, or Darktable
- A clear "stop editing" signal when further tweaking would hurt

Post 6 (CTA):
It's free to try — no credit card.

[Link]

If you're a photographer who wants honest, structured feedback,
I'd love to hear what you think.
```

## Writing Rules

**Source:** Corey Haines, marketingskills (MIT), copywriting and email-sequence skills, 2026 — rules 1-4 below are adapted closely from the copywriting skill's writing-style rules, and rules 5 and 8 from the email-sequence skill's guidelines.

1. **Simple over complex.** "Use" not "utilize." "Help" not "facilitate."
2. **Specific over vague.** Avoid "streamline," "optimize," "innovative," "leverage."
3. **Active over passive.** "We analyze your photo" not "Your photo is analyzed."
4. **Confident over qualified.** Remove "almost," "very," "really," "quite."
5. **Short paragraphs.** 1-3 sentences max. White space is your friend.
6. **One idea per paragraph.** Don't stack multiple arguments in one block.
7. **Use "you" and "your."** Talk to the reader, not about "users."
8. **Read it out loud.** If it sounds robotic or stilted, rewrite it.
