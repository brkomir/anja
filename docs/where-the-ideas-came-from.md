# Credits: what was built here, and what Anja adopted from other projects

Anja was built inside one application, the source project, starting in March 2026, as answers to that application's own problems: written rules where a mistake repeated, hooks where a rule was not enough, a memory that survives the session, and review by a session that did not write the change. Most of it was designed and written there, in daily use, and it evolved through the demands of that work.

From April 2026 other agent systems were studied as well, and specific ideas were adopted from them between April and September; the table below gives each source, the date it was first studied, what was taken and what it became here. On 2026-10-09, after most of Anja was built, a read-only sweep of 52 similar systems placed it among its neighbours.

This page records that history in three parts: the main mechanisms built here, the ideas adopted from other projects, and where Anja sits among similar systems.

## Built here

These mechanisms were designed and written here, and no outside source is recorded for their core; where a part was adopted from elsewhere, its file credits it in place (the review hub's scope slot is one). Each line ends with what the [evidence table](evidence.md) found for it. [The comparison with 52 systems below](#where-this-sits-among-its-neighbours), read on 2026-10-09, names the nearest relatives; none of these lines claims that no other system does something similar.

- **Promoting a guard from warn to block on its own logged fires.** A new guard only warns and logs each time it fires, and is promoted to refusing on that log. The one mechanism the reading of 52 systems below did not find in this form elsewhere. Evidence: operated, and individual promotions are recorded. The discipline itself has no before/after, and one arm promoted to block later false-fired on real work.
- **The hook smoke suite and its mutation matrices**, which drive the guards with arms that should fire and arms that should stay quiet, and test whether each assertion can fail. Run at pre-push and in CI in the source project; the copy ships neither the pre-push hook nor a CI workflow. Evidence: mixed. A hook-level pre-pass found 5 of 37 hooks whose assertions could not fail (a separate count from the five assertions the matrix's own first version wrongly reported as inert), and several self-tests were later found unable to fail.
- **Scoping the rules by path, and a plane of doctrine for the main session only.** The `paths:` key is the harness's own; the split of the rulebook and the rigs that measured it were built here. Evidence: positive. A rule that does not match is left out of a sub-agent's payload, and doctrine only the main session can act on is delivered to the main session, not to a sub-agent when it starts.
- **The exhortation cut**, a pre-registered experiment that removed persuasive prose from the always-loaded rules in three rounds while keeping every hook and test. Superpowers made a similar cut first and measured it with control arms (its row below). Evidence: two reads detected no effect on its primary measure, which could detect only a rise of about 15 points; the second read left two signals unresolved.
- **Byte guards on agent memory**, on top of the harness's own auto-memory. Evidence: when the read guard refused an unbounded read and returned paging coordinates, agents recovered by paging 68.1% of the time, against 12.7% after a plain byte-cap error: an observational comparison, not a randomised one.
- **Session mail and its listener.** The listener runs on the harness's `Monitor` tool, and the harness has since added messaging between sessions of its own. Evidence: 92.3% of direct mail delivered to a running session (15,472 of 16,754, 16 August to 20 October 2026); whether each was read was not measured.
- **The review hub**, a standing session that reviews the commits its peer sessions mail to it. Review in a context that did not write the change is common (Superpowers reviews each task in a fresh subagent); the standing cross-session seat is what was built here. Evidence: not measured.
- **The first-turn echo fix.** Evidence: 167 sessions after the fix showed no fabricated first reply; the scan rules out the previous rate at 95% confidence and stops short of calling the defect fixed.

The evidence table also records two things built here that did not work as intended: a byte budget on always-loaded text whose ceiling kept being raised, and drift sentinels whose recorded findings are about the sentinels' own blind spots.

## Adopted between April and September 2026

| `wan-huiyan/agent-review-panel` | 2026-04-18 | From its adversarial review panel: a targeted re-check when two reviewers dispute a finding; a skepticism level for each reviewer; a rule that a verdict changed under pushback needs new evidence; labels saying how well a finding is supported, two of which are kept here; tags saying whether a claim about the outside world was checked on the web; a written process log for large panels; a flag for human review when the synthesis has low confidence; and its view that "unanimous agreement is the most dangerous failure mode", with the correlation notice that states it | In `/expert-review`. ## Where this sits among its neighbours

On 2026-10-09, after most of Anja was built, a broad read-only sweep compared it with 52 similar systems, read nearly all at their primary sources (star counts from that day), to see where it sits. Many of its mechanisms turned out to have relatives among them, built separately. The closest, by mechanism:

- **Peer mail on one shared branch.** MCP Agent Mail (2,198★): git-tracked markdown mail with read and ack receipts, file reservations with a TTL and a pre-commit guard against a live reservation, the closest to the copy's mail, file reservations between sessions and commit checks. Gas Town (18,310★) adds a coordinator.
- **A review gate bound to a change hash.** Loom (cosmix, 61★) ties its gate to a fingerprint of the change. Temper (17★) and cairn-framework (46★) bind a verdict to the sha256 of what it reviewed. Edda (36★) refuses a reviewer whose session wrote the commits and voids a review once the base moves.
- **A measured lesson lifecycle.** keel-harness (2★) turns a failure recurring across runs into a blocking check once a human promotes it and a runnable oracle exists. stet.sh (5★, closed; per its docs) promotes or holds a rule-file change on replayed, held-out evidence.
- **Session retrospection.** retro-skill (13★) runs a scripted friction pre-pass before the LLM retrospective and routes findings to seven destinations, one of them the upstream source rather than a memory entry. Edda's post-mortem learns rules and fades unused ones.
- **Durable task state.** Tusk's (1★) SQLite task model and valkor-ai/loom's (1,211★) revision-checked delivery state are closest to the copy's working plans.

Not found in this form: a warn-then-block promotion earned from a rule's own logged fires. Several of the 52 carry parts of it, keel-harness and stet.sh the closest. A reading of 52 systems, not a claim about the field.

Many mechanisms also rest on Claude Code's own features; [how Anja's mechanisms compare with Claude Code's own features](beside-native-features.md) says which, and where the harness now does the job itself.

## How credit is recorded

The 52 systems in the section above were read to see where Anja sits, not to adopt from them. Smaller adoptions are credited only in the file that carries them. A finding raised by one of this system's own review seats is this system's work, also when the seat is modelled on an outside system. Text copied or adapted from another project is a separate record, credited with its licence in `.claude/NOTICE` and traced file by file in `.claude/third-party-origins.md`.
