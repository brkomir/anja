# Anja evidence: what was measured

Anja is the engineering system the maintainer built and used from March to October 2026 to develop the source project, a photography critique application, and ship it to production as a solo developer. Its mechanisms were built in response to that work and ran in it. This page sets out what the operational record from those months demonstrates, mechanism by mechanism, and where the evidence stops.

What the record shows:

- **The system ran in production work.** Every mechanism family in the table below operated in the source project, not only in a design note, and most were instrumented while they ran: fire logs, ledgers, event logs and pre-registered experiment notes all come from that daily use.
- **Mechanism tests that hold.** Path scoping, and the delivery property the main-session doctrine relies on, were measured in throwaway-repo rigs and hold. Mail delivery was measured: 92.3% of direct mail reached a running session; whether mail is read was not measured. The read guard has a positive reading that is observational. The first-turn echo fix reads positive within its stated bound, and the swarm mode's validation gate passed on its due date.
- **Null and negative results.** The exhortation cut and the lesson loop's gate analysis found no detectable effect. Several instruments were found failing: self-tests that could not fail, fire logs full of the test suite's own fixtures, a byte budget whose ceiling kept being raised, and a commit gate that narrows, and does not close, the window it guards. Each is recorded below beside the positive results.
- **Where the evidence stops.** There is no controlled comparison of whole-project outcomes against unmodified Claude Code, and no before/after for defect rate, rework or wrong assertions across the system; several mechanisms, independent review among them, have no effect read. [Evidence boundaries and open measurements](#evidence-boundaries-and-open-measurements) lists each one.

Each mechanism family is classified on seven axes, in five columns:

- **operated**: it ran in a real project, not only in a design note;
- **instrumented**: something counted its fires or its outcomes;
- **effect measured**: a before/after or a controlled read exists;
- **effect not measured**: no such read exists, whatever the prose around it says;
- **positive result** / **null or negative result**: what the read found;
- **superseded by native tooling**: the harness has something that does the same job, whether it shipped before or after the mechanism.

Every figure below comes from a file in this copy, named where it is used, or from the project's experiment notes, which are outside this tree and are marked as such. Two caveats apply to everything that follows:

- **You cannot re-derive the fire counts.** The hooks' fire logs and the review ledger live in directories the copy never lists, so a reader of the copy can check how a mechanism is built but not how often it fired. Every count of fires quoted here is a reading somebody took, copied into a comment or a document.
- **Inventory counts are not evidence.** How many hooks, rules or lessons exist says nothing about whether any of them works. They appear only where a measurement needs a denominator.

"Lecture N" and "Appendix X" are sections of `.claude/docs/architecture.md`; an "arm" is one pattern inside a guard.

## What each measured row found

**Hook guards.**
- **Kill-command arm.** `.claude/hooks/block-dangerous.sh` blocks a `pkill`/`pgrep`/`killall` with an option after its pattern (the arm dates from 2026-10-05). Before it shipped, its header records a replay over 908 real invocations from every transcript on the machine: 4 fires, all 4 the true shape, and 0 fires on 203 commands that only mentioned a kill tool.
- **Grep-blowup guard (`.claude/hooks/grep-blowup-warn.sh`, shipped unwired).** Lecture 5 and `session-cadence.md` record that on 2026-08-01 its absolute-root arm took 3 of 3 false positives, all of them mentions of a path rather than invocations. The fix was to leave that arm at warn and the other arm at block. `session-cadence.md` gives its ledger as 1,598 records: 269 deny, 1,307 warn, 22 with no decision. The ledger carries no classification field, so a false-positive rate cannot be computed from it.
- **Author-filter arm.** `.claude/hooks/bash-safety-warn.sh` was calibrated on 2026-09-07 over 50 logged fires: 22 `--author-date-order`, 4 real `--author=`, and 24 unreadable because the logged command was capped at 400 bytes. A first fix treated the 22 as false positives. A review then found them to be the arm's main true positives.
- **Test-fixture pollution.** `.claude/hooks/lib/fires-log.sh` records that on 2026-09-13, 50 of the 51 records in the push guard's fire log were the smoke suite's own fixtures. `always-loaded-claim-warn.sh` records the same for its bash-write log: 1,422 of 1,437 rows written between 2026-09-13 and 2026-10-09 were fixtures. `block-subagent-always-loaded-write.sh` notes that its plain-text log held 11,320 lines over the same period, against 50 live denies in its JSONL log. The suite now redirects logs written through `lib/fires-log.sh`; the push guard's main deny path still writes its own log, kept clean only where a suite arm sets `PUSH_GUARD_NO_LOG=1`.
- **Delivery facts.** Two facts about delivery were measured, and both shape how every hook in the system is written. `bash-environment.md`: a hook that emits two JSON documents had both canaries vanish, while a single document arrived verbatim. `session-cadence.md`, 2026-09-21: a PreToolUse denial reached a dispatched sub-agent's tool call, measured on one hook and one agent type.

**Always-loaded rules and path scoping.**
- **First measurement (experiment notes).** On 2026-08-17 the rule files totalled 503,167 bytes. Of those, 189,367 were delivered to every sub-agent dispatch and 313,800 could be scoped by path. Path scoping (the `paths:` frontmatter) was shown to drop an unmatched rule from a dispatched agent's payload, and the instructions-loaded hook corroborated it at run time.
- **Lecture 4, 2026-09-14.** The three resident rule files came to 193,340 of 684,535 bytes, so a session that touches none of the scoped surfaces is spared about 72% of the rule bytes. The pilot file, `tests-unit.md` at 66,186 bytes, was missing from every session-start load in a session that never touched `tests/`.
- **Three measuring traps (same lecture).** Counting path-match loads values the mechanism backwards, because each such load is a case where scoping saved nothing. Counting absences overstates the saving by about 2.3×. And presence fades: 4 of 7 compactions re-injected a scoped file, and 3 did not.
- **Two over-wide globs were narrowed.** `tests-unit.md` records that its broad glob had delivered unit-test rules to integration and e2e readers on 50 of 285 measured loads before it was narrowed on 2026-09-04. `session-cadence.md` records that its old glob delivered 27 KB to 115 of 179 sub-agents in one day, all on a memory read the rules could not help with.
- **What these figures cover.** The byte figures were measured on the orchestrator only (the main session). On 2026-10-10 the live rule set was 615,113 bytes over 19 files (experiment notes).

**Orchestrator plane.** Lecture 4 records that on 2026-09-03 every non-fork sub-agent started with 358,986 bytes of always-loaded text, 94% of its starting context, and about 101 KB of that was doctrine only the main session could act on. That doctrine moved to `.claude/orchestrator/`. The move rests on a property measured in a throwaway repo: a SessionStart hook's output reaches the main session and not a dispatched sub-agent at launch. The one exception is a sub-agent's own compaction, which re-fires SessionStart inside the agent. The copy gives no figure for a sub-agent's payload after the move.

**Exhortation cut.** From the project's experiment notes, outside this tree:
- **What was cut.** A pre-registered experiment begun on 2026-09-07 removed persuasive rule prose from the always-loaded rulebook in three rounds (2026-09-07; 2026-09-09 for the orchestrator doctrine; 2026-09-23). Every hook, gate and test stayed in place.
- **The read.** On 2026-09-21 the pre-registered read found that the measured verify-before-assert rate had moved +4.2 points against a ±5-point band, so the cut stood.
- **Round three's read.** On 2026-10-09 the same rate rose 2.6 points, inside the 5-point band. Because a rise in a second kind of entry, preference violations (corrections for acting against a preference the project had already recorded), pushes that share down by itself, the plan also registered the same rate counted only among the other entries; it rose 7.5 points, which the plan calls ambiguous. When the two land in different bands the plan's rule is to extend. The extension was cancelled instead: new entries had slowed to about a quarter of the window's pace, so a later read would not have resolved materially more, and the experiment was closed with the cut kept and that signal unresolved, along with a second one: a rarer kind of correction (giving up a position under pushback) went from none to two entries, too few to read.
- **Two thresholds, both from the plan.** The experiment pre-registered a band of 5 points inside which the cut would stand and a move of more than 10 points that would revert it; Lecture 4 of the architecture copy names only the second. A move between the two extends the measurement. The 2026-09-21 read landed inside the first; round three's read put its main measure inside the first as well and its second share between the two.
- **What led to the cut.** The trigger fire-rate extract listed 27 triggers that had never fired (Lecture 4).

**Memory tiers and read guards.**
- **How often agents read their memory (2026-08-01).** The header of `.claude/hooks/warn-large-tracker-read.sh` records an audit of 4,184 sub-agent dispatches: 356 reads hit the per-call cap, and 243 of those (68%) never paged further. In the hard-byte class, 229 errors were followed by only 29 recoveries, so 87.3% of those agents worked without their own memory.
- **Silent truncation.** The header of `scripts/agent-memory-load-audit.mjs` records that on the same day, 638 of 1,643 apparently successful first reads (38.8%) were actually truncated with no error.
- **A re-measurement with a fixed classifier (Lecture 6, 2026-08-26).** The "never attempted the read" share was revised from 39% over 4,184 dispatches to 27.6% over 6,953 dispatches, plus 5.1% that read an alternate path. The original figure had been counting an instrument artifact along with the real failure.
- **The one effect read in this family.** The same audit header records that when the read guard denied an unbounded read and handed back paging coordinates, agents recovered by paging 68.1% of the time, against 12.7% after a plain byte-cap error. The header says this shows the guard is not inert. It is an observational comparison between two failure classes, not a randomised one, and the copy gives no date for it.
- **Naming the memory path in a brief.** Appendix B records the basis for a hook that ships unwired (`.claude/hooks/agent-memory-brief-warn.sh`): a brief that names the agent's memory path gets it read 98 to 100% of the time (0 of 39 never attempted), while a brief that does not leaves 33.0% of agents without their memory. This too is observational.

**Lesson loop.** The analysis was built to compare each pushback tag's share of the corrections registry before and after a decision-time gate for it shipped. Appendix C records the outcome: the share fell for a gate whose remedy is a concrete check and rose for a gate whose remedy is restraint, but both shifts fell below the analysis's own minimum sample and were withheld. So the lesson rests on that qualitative split, not on a significant measurement. Lecture 8 also records a compression that did not hold: a distillation on 2026-05-22 cut the meta memory from 2,052 lines to 1,294, and it was back at 1,682 by 2026-05-23.

**Smoke suite, self-tests and mutation matrices.**
- **Size and cost.** The header of `scripts/smoke-test-hooks.sh` records 1,444 to 1,448 checks taking 3m34s to 3m48s locally (2026-09-14). As a CI step it took about 103 s, which Lecture 5 places at 61% of a 168 s job (2026-09-01, when the suite was 7,153 lines; it is about 13,100 lines in the copy).
- **Which assertions can fail.** The header of `scripts/smoke-mutation-matrix.sh` frames its question over 388 green assertions. An earlier pre-pass, which killed each hook with one of 37 mutations, found 32 hooks guarded by assertions that go red and 5 hooks whose assertions could not fail. The matrix's own first version wrongly reported five assertions as inert, because it matched assertions to hooks by section name.
- **Instruments that misled.** `bash-environment.md` records that on 2026-08-26 two hand-runs of the matrix, made while a long run was live, produced two false "unguarded" verdicts and roughly 6× inflation on every other verdict, because they shared one scratch file. `session-cadence.md` records a guard whose deletion scored 48 of 48 passing, because no test arm could reach it. A related probe found that mutating a guard nested two closures deep turned 7 of 50 red, while mutating a module-scope function left all 50 green; the rule's conclusion is that whether a test could reach the code, not how deeply it was nested, decided which went red.
- **Self-tests that could not fail.** `scripts/deploy-env-drift.mjs` printed "4/4 PASS" while the logic it was meant to cover had zero arms. `scripts/meta-corrections-scan.py` shipped new alternatives with no arms, and its pass count stayed at 10. `scripts/wrap-reconcile.mjs` computed one strip inline, where no self-test arm could see it, until 2026-09-15. The smoke suite's own header records that on 2026-09-07 an escape variable leaking in from settings left the 33 assertions for the per-turn orchestrator-rules hook (`band-o-inject.sh`) reading empty.

**Session mail.** The listener became a standing directive on 2026-07-19. `.claude/hooks/session-start.sh` and Lecture 9 record the figure it graduated on: 89% of mail marked read (1,447 of 1,618), 160 receipts from live surfacing, and an addressable gap of about 0.6%. Most of that mail was marked read when a session started. The same hook records why that count did not measure reading: one session start emitted 147,083 bytes of mail, of which 2,174 bytes (1.48%) reached context, and no message bytes arrived at all, yet all 48 read receipts were written. Since 2026-08-14 a session start shows subject lines only and marks nothing read, so the 89% is not quoted here as a reading figure. What is measured is delivery: from 16 August to 20 October 2026, 15,472 of 16,754 direct mails (92.3%) were delivered by the listener to a running session, counted from the metadata-only event log. Whether each delivered mail was read was not measured.

**Fleet coordination.** Lecture 7 records that the swarm mode's validation gate fired on its due date, 2026-08-06, and passed: 0 of 16 mis-routed against a bar of under 10%, with 1 duplicate that the system caught itself.

**Post-compact recovery.** `.claude/hooks/session-start.sh` records that no session transcript contains a single attachment from the native PostCompact hook. An earlier check that seemed to show it firing had only matched the investigation's own echoes. Recovery therefore moved to SessionStart's compact branch, and it now leaves a marker that keeps nagging, after a one-shot directive was recorded failing silently twice (Lecture 5). The same hook gives the checkpoint replay's size, median 1,370 bytes and maximum 6,929. How often recovery actually happens is not measured.

**First-turn echo fix.**
- **The defect.** `.claude/hooks/session-start.sh` records five sessions whose first reply was a fabricated rules document instead of the typed command.
- **Why it can't be partitioned.** The fix is a hypothesis. In this corpus the date and the CLI version move together by construction (0 off-diagonal across 148 sessions), so only the intervention can test it.
- **The read (experiment notes).** After the fix, the echo scan counted 167 sessions with 0 rendered echoes (read 2026-10-10). The scan's own text says this rules out the previously measured rate at 95% confidence, and it stops short of calling the defect fixed.

**Drift sentinels.**
- **Predicates that could never match.** `session-cadence.md` records that on 2026-08-26 the shape check in `scripts/defer-trigger-scan.py` admitted 13 of 131 trigger predicates that matched none of 6,529 tracked files. The first fix only took that to 8.
- **A drift the sync tool could not see.** Lecture 8 records that a line count drifted by 835 lines while `architecture-counts.mjs check` read clean, because its drift detector did not match that phrasing.
- **A trigger that could not fire.** Lecture 11 records that one of the two triggers for the doc-audit cadence step is structurally zero since the meta memory became a router.

**Byte budget.** Lecture 4 records the ceiling raised three times in four days (330 to 400 to 440 KB), then from 473 to 825 KB across seven more raises. The lecture's own conclusion is that a limit that moves this often is not a control. The same lecture records that from 2026-09-19 to 2026-10-11 a settings switch turned the gate off for every session's push.

**Meta self-audit.** Lecture 8 records that a stale check count turned up as soon as a new check for entry-point prose ran. It also records that a maintenance run once reported a clean inventory while the always-loaded band sat 13 KB over its ceiling, which is why a fourteenth check was added. A due-date gate was retired after three consecutive runs in which everything was due.

## Evidence boundaries and open measurements

The measurements above test mechanisms one at a time, in the project where they ran. What follows is what they do not cover: the comparative benchmark that has not been run, and the mechanisms that ran without an effect read. These are limits on what can be claimed by comparison; they do not undo the record of use above.

- **A controlled comparison of the whole system.** No controlled, head-to-head measurement compares whole-project outcomes with the system against unmodified Claude Code: no before/after exists for defect rate, rework or wrong assertions across the system. The one pre-registered measurement of system-wide behaviour, the exhortation cut (an observational before/after, not a controlled trial), detected no effect from removing a large body of prose rules, on a measure that could detect only a rise of about 15 points, so a smaller effect is neither shown nor ruled out; two signals from its second read were left unresolved when the experiment was closed.
- **Independent review.** This project has not measured how many defects a fresh-context reviewer catches compared with the builder. The architecture document itself notes that builder and reviewer usually come from the same model family and share blind spots.
- **Two adherence figures with no measurement behind them.** Lecture 5 says rules-only enforcement reaches "roughly 85–90% adherence", and Lecture 7 says a convention is "ignored ~10-15% of the time". The copy gives neither a date nor a measurement for either.
- **The share of lessons that come from pushback.** Lecture 9's "~30%" is labelled informal, undated and not re-derivable.
- **Other mechanisms with no effect read.** The model-routing policy, the sniff-test, the meta self-audit and the commit safety gates have never been measured for effect, and neither has the post-compact recovery rate.
- **Whether a session checks your decision log before it recommends a change.** Nothing that loads in every session asks it to. The rule that did, a row of `active-recall.md`, is parked with the other prose rules, and the source project's security rules, which stated its specific past decisions outright, are not in the copy. Three skills ask for the lookup, and only while they run: the expert-review panel (before it dispatches and again in its synthesis checklist), `uncle-bob` before it reports a finding, and `context-architect` before it presents an option. Nothing has measured whether a session does it.
- **Fire counts.** As noted at the top, the fire logs and the review ledger do not ship, so no count on this page can be re-derived from the copy.
